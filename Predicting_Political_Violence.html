Data Exploration and Preparation

Data Preparation

In order to make sure that I was able to train my models on the best subset of data possible, I had to read through the PIRUS Codebook to develop an understanding of what each variable in the dataset meant, and consider what that variable might mean for my models. For starters, I filtered out all observations that held “NA” values. Of the 124 variables in the original, only 10 are numeric, and each variable that held “NA” values was a categorical variable. Performing imputation on these variables would have been difficult and could have negatively influenced my model, so for the time being I figured it was best to just remove them from the training data. I also removed variables related to the violent plots of the extremists, as these values would have been directly informative of my response variable. Lastly, I removed variables like SubjectID and the social network nodes, as these are irrelevant to the goal of predicting whether or not an extremist will participate in or commit an act of political violence.

political_extremism <- pirus_fct %>%
  filter(Radicalization_Islamist != "Yes") %>%
  filter(Ideological_Sub_Category1 != "Christian Identity" & Ideological_Sub_Category1 != "Islamist" & Ideological_Sub_Category1 != "Jewish Defense Lg.") %>% # Because I'm just focusing on politically-motivated extremism and not religiously-motivated extremism, I'm removing people with religious ideological sub categories.
  select(-c(Subject_ID, Loc_Plot_State1:Loc_Plot_City2, Plot_Target1:Extent_Plot, Primary_Event_Mass_Casualty_Incident:Foreign_Fighter, Component_ID, Component_Density, Degree_Centrality:Closeness_Centrality)) %>% 
  mutate(Violent = case_when(
# Have to make Violent a binomial variable for a logistic regression
    Violent == "Yes" ~ 1,
    Violent == "No" ~ 0
  ))# This removes all of the variables related to plots and the social network nodes of an extremist
